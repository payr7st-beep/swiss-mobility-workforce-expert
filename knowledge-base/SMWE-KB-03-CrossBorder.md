# SMWE Knowledge Base — Module 03: Cross-Border Work

**Version:** 1.0 | **Effective:** 1 August 2026 | **Next review:** 1 November 2026  
**Service codes:** GM-002, GM-003, IMM-005  
**Legal sources:** AFMP, AIG, VZAE, EU Reg. 883/2004, bilateral DTAs (CH-DE 1971, CH-FR 1966+2023, CH-IT 1976+2023), SEM Directives

---

## 1. Definition: Cross-Border Worker

A cross-border worker (Grenzgänger / travailleur frontalier / lavoratore frontaliero) is a person who:
- Works in Switzerland
- Resides in a neighbouring country
- Returns to their country of residence at least once per week (G permit condition)

**Permit:** G Permit (cross-border commuter permit / Grenzgängerausweis)

---

## 2. Social Security: Universal Threshold Rule

**EU Regulation 883/2004 (via AFMP) — Principle of one applicable legislation:**

A cross-border worker is subject to the social security legislation of ONE state only — generally the state where they work (lex loci laboris).

**Critical threshold:** If a cross-border worker performs **≥25% of their working time** in their home country (telework, home office, client visits), a multi-state analysis is required.

**Hard shift threshold:** If the worker performs **≥50% (i.e. more than 49.9%) of their working time** in their country of residence → social security shifts to the country of residence.

**Impact of shift on Swiss employer:**
- Swiss AHV/ALV/BVG contributions cease
- Employer must register and contribute to the foreign social security authority
- Significant administrative and cost impact
- Worker loses Swiss social security entitlements

## 3. Country-Specific Rules

### 3.1 France

**Social Security (EU Reg. 883/2004):**
- As long as home-country (France) work time stays below 49.9%, Swiss coverage maintained
- Monitoring threshold: 25% soft trigger → notify ZAS; formal multi-state form required
- A1 required for all work performed in France

**Income Tax — DTA Franco-Suisse 1966 + Protocol 2023:**

*Geneva canton (special regime):*
- French residents working in Geneva historically taxed in France under special border-canton regime
- Protocol 2023: maintained for existing workers; new arrangements may differ
- Employer must apply correct at-source withholding rules for Geneva cross-border workers

*Other cantons (Vaud, Valais, Neuchâtel, Bern, Basel, etc.):*
- Standard DTA Article 15 applies
- Income taxed in Switzerland (at source = Quellensteuer) for Swiss-working days
- Home-country (French) working days taxed in France

*Telework agreement (2023):*
- Specific bilateral agreement allowing up to **40% remote work** without changing tax status
- Below 40%: French resident remains taxed in Switzerland on full income
- Above 40%: French tax rights expand proportionately
- Employer must track and document day-by-day work location

**Risk level:** 🟢 Low (with clear framework and < 40% home office)

---

### 3.2 Germany

**Social Security (EU Reg. 883/2004):**
- Standard 49.9% threshold applies
- No formal home-office safe harbour for social security purposes
- Even single days of work in Germany theoretically require documentation
- A1 required for all Germany work days

**Income Tax — DTA Deutschland-Schweiz 1971 (revised):**

**Physical presence principle:** Every day physically present in Germany and working is taxable in Germany.

**No formal threshold:** Unlike France, there is no agreed percentage of days that protect Swiss taxation rights over home-working days.

**Consequence:** A German-resident employee who works any days from home in Germany has proportional income tax liability in Germany.

**Salary split payroll:** Industry standard response. The salary is split:
- Swiss portion (Swiss working days): taxed in Switzerland via Quellensteuer
- German portion (German working days): subject to German wage tax (Lohnsteuer)

**Risk level:** 🔴 High (strict enforcement; salary split typically required above any material home-office activity)

**Key thresholds for Germany:**
- **183-day rule (Article 15 DTA):** If employee spends ≥183 days in Germany in a calendar year, Germany taxes all employment income → major escalation trigger
- **Social security shift:** >49.9% working time in Germany → full shift to Deutsche Rentenversicherung / German system

---

### 3.3 Italy

**Old Frontaliers (pre-17 July 2023 — original CH-IT agreement):**
- Swiss-source income taxed **only in Switzerland**
- Must return home daily or very frequently
- Protected status under prior agreement

**New Frontaliers (from 17 July 2023 — revised CH-IT agreement 2023):**
- **Concurrent taxation:** income taxed in **both Switzerland and Italy**
- Switzerland: reduced withholding tax (80% of normal rate) on a portion
- Italy: income reportable and taxable with credit for Swiss tax
- Annual reconciliation required on Italian tax return

**Telework threshold (new frontaliers):**
- **25% maximum** home-office time before social security review
- Exceeding 25% home-country work time → multi-state A1 analysis required

**Risk level:** 🟡 Medium (new agreement creates dual-tax complexity; strict tracking required)

---

### 3.4 Austria

**Social Security:** EU Reg. 883/2004 applies; standard 49.9% threshold.

**Income Tax — DTA Österreich-Schweiz 2000:**
- Article 15: employment income taxed in state of physical work performance
- No special frontier-worker regime; standard DTA rules apply
- Salary split required for home-office days if material

**Risk level:** 🟡 Medium (standard DTA; less case law than CH-DE or CH-FR)

## 4. A1 Certificate for Cross-Border Workers

**All cross-border workers** holding a G permit and performing **any work in their country of residence** require an A1 certificate.

**A1 for multi-state workers (Art. 13 EU Reg. 883/2004):**
- Applicable when employee works in two or more EU/EFTA states habitually
- Form: A1 confirming applicable legislation (typically Switzerland if <50% home-country)
- Application: cantonal AHV compensation office / ALPS system
- Must be renewed annually or when circumstances change

**A1 for business trips from Switzerland:**
- Required for each EU/EFTA country visited
- One blanket A1 can cover multi-country travel for regular travellers
- Country-specific rules may require advance notification

## 5. Working Day Tracking for Cross-Border Workers

### Minimum documentation standard:

| Record type | Minimum | Best practice |
|---|---|---|
| Travel diary | Daily log of work location | Digital calendar with location tags |
| Badge/access records | Office access swipes | Electronic building access log |
| VPN/login logs | Remote access records | IT-sourced geolocation data |
| Expense records | Hotel/transport receipts | Integrated expense management |

### Threshold monitoring calendar:

| Monitoring point | Action |
|---|---|
| Home-country days reach 20% | Warning flag; review employee setup |
| Home-country days reach 25% | Formal multi-state A1 assessment required |
| Home-country days reach 40% | Tax impact review (France specific); all countries DTA review |
| Home-country days reach 49% | Urgent: social security shift imminent |
| Home-country days exceed 50% | Social security shift triggered; register abroad |

## 6. Permanent Establishment Risk

Regular home-office work in the worker's country of residence may create a **fixed place of business** (fester Ort der Geschäftstätigkeit / place fixe d'affaires) for the Swiss employer in the foreign country.

**Triggers for PE concern (OECD Model Art. 5):**
- Employee conducts commercial activities from home (client meetings, contract negotiation)
- Employer provides dedicated home-office equipment and formally requires home work
- Activity is habitual and regular, not incidental

**Swiss employer's exposure:**
- Corporate income tax filing obligation in worker's home country
- Registration as foreign employer
- Local payroll obligations

**Risk mitigation:**
- Cap home-office days at ≤40% (stricter than social security threshold)
- Adopt clear home-office policy: "incidental" character; no commercial negotiations from home
- Consider obtaining advance tax rulings (Verbindliche Auskunft / ruling fiscal) in both countries

## 7. Posted Workers Inbound to Switzerland

Foreign employers sending workers to perform services in Switzerland must comply with:

**EntsG (Posted Workers Act) obligations:**
- 8-day advance online notification via SECO platform
- Minimum wages (GAV or statutory minimum where applicable)
- Maximum working hours (ArG)
- Minimum annual leave
- Equal treatment in accommodation and meals expenses

**Documentation to retain on-site:**
- Employment contract (in a language auditors can read)
- Proof of social security coverage (A1 certificate or equivalent)
- Payslips for period of posting
- Working time records

**Enforcement:** SECO and cantonal labour inspectorates conduct random checks. Fines for non-compliance: up to CHF 5,000 per individual; repeat violations up to CHF 40,000.
