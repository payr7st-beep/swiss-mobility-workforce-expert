## 1. Swiss DTA Network Overview

Switzerland has **over 100 Double Taxation Agreements (DTAs)** in force globally as of 1 January 2026.

**Official source:** State Secretariat for International Finance (SIF) — "Swiss Double Taxation Agreements – Status 1 January 2026"

**Key purpose:** DTAs prevent the same income being taxed by two countries simultaneously. They allocate taxing rights between Switzerland and the treaty partner.

## 2. Key DTA Articles for Mobility & Workforce

### Article 4 — Residence (Domicile fiscal / Steuerdomizil)
Determines where a person is **tax resident** — the foundation of all DTA analysis.

**Individual residence tie-breaker (OECD Model Art. 4(2)):**
In order of priority:
1. Permanent home (Wohnsitz / domicile permanent)
2. Centre of vital interests (wirtschaftliche und persönliche Bindungen / centre des intérêts vitaux)
3. Habitual abode (gewöhnlicher Aufenthalt / séjour habituel)
4. Nationality
5. Mutual agreement between competent authorities

**Swiss domestic rule for residence:** Switzerland taxes on worldwide income if domicile (Art. 3 DBG) or 30-day habitual stay with gainful activity.

### Article 15 — Employment Income

**Default rule:** Employment income taxed in the **state where work is performed** (source state = physical presence).

**183-day exemption (Art. 15(2)):** Home-state taxation maintained if ALL THREE conditions met:
1. Employee is not present in the host state for more than **183 days** in any 12-month period (or calendar year, depending on DTA text)
2. Employer paying the remuneration is **not resident** in the host state
3. The remuneration is **not borne by a permanent establishment** of the employer in the host state

**If all three conditions are met:** Only the state of residence may tax (even though work is performed in the other state).

**If any one condition fails:** Host-state may tax income for working days in host state.

### Article 16 — Directors' Fees
Directors' fees (Verwaltungsratshonorar / tantième de conseil d'administration) are taxed in the state where the company is resident. This is an exception to Art. 15.

### Article 18/19 — Pensions
Private pensions: generally taxed only in state of residence.
Government pensions: generally taxed in the state that pays them.

### Article 5 — Permanent Establishment
A PE in a country gives that country the right to tax business profits attributable to the PE. Relevant for employers with home-office employees abroad.

## 3. Priority Treaty Partners for Swiss Mobility

### CH-DE (Germany) — DTA 1971, revised
- **Art. 15:** Salary for days worked in Germany taxed in Germany; days in Switzerland taxed in Switzerland
- **Frontier workers (Grenzgänger):** Article 15(4) — special regime. German-resident cross-border workers working primarily in Switzerland taxed in Switzerland, minus a deduction (German municipalities retain 4.5%)
- **183-day rule:** Calendar year based in CH-DE
- **Enforcement:** Very strict German tax enforcement; large penalty regime

### CH-FR (France) — DTA 1966 + Protocol 2023
- **Art. 15:** Employment income → state of work
- **Frontier workers:** Complex border-canton regime (Geneva: France taxes; other cantons: Switzerland taxes)
- **Telework protocol (2023):** Up to 40% home-office without changing DTA position
- **183-day rule:** Calendar year based

### CH-IT (Italy) — DTA 1976, revised 2023
- **Old frontaliers (pre-17 July 2023):** Switzerland-only taxation
- **New frontaliers (post-17 July 2023):** Concurrent taxation; Italy taxes up to 80% of Swiss-taxed amount
- **Standard employees (non-frontaliers):** Art. 15 applies
- **183-day rule:** 12-month period based

### CH-AT (Austria) — DTA 2000
- **Art. 15:** Standard employment income rule
- **No special frontier-worker article**
- **183-day rule:** Calendar year based

### CH-UK — DTA 1977 (post-Brexit status)
- **Still in force** post-Brexit (bilateral DTA separate from EU law)
- **Art. 15:** Standard employment income rule
- **183-day rule:** Calendar year based
- **Note:** UK left EU social security coordination; new bilateral social security protocol applies separately

### CH-US — DTA 1996 + Protocol 2009
- **US taxes its citizens on worldwide income regardless of DTA** — US citizens are taxed by the US even in Switzerland
- **FICA (Social Security + Medicare):** US/Switzerland totalization agreement applies; separate from DTA
- **183-day rule:** Calendar year based; US enforcement through FBAR and FATCA reporting
- **Swiss withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer):** 35%; reduced to 15% (dividends) or 5% (controlling stakes) under DTA

### CH-IN (India) — DTA 1994
- **Art. 15:** Standard employment income rule
- **183-day rule:** Calendar year based
- **India withholding:** Indian employers withholding on India-source income
- **Social security:** Bilateral agreement allows 60-month posting

### CH-CN (China) — DTA 2013
- **Art. 15:** Standard employment income rule
- **183-day rule:** Calendar year based
- **Social security:** 60-month bilateral agreement

## 4. The 183-Day Rule — Practical Application

### What counts as a day?
- Generally: any day of physical presence in the host state counts (even a partial day — arrival and departure may each count as one day under some DTAs)
- **Switzerland/Germany DTA:** departure and arrival days count separately
- Check specific DTA text; some treaties explicitly state otherwise

### What the 183-day rule does NOT do:
- It does not eliminate withholding obligations on the employer in many countries
- It does not protect against social security obligations (separate rules apply)
- It does not protect against PE risk for the employer

### Year calculation:
- **Calendar year:** most Swiss DTAs use 1 Jan to 31 Dec
- **12-month period:** some DTAs use any rolling 12-month period (more complex; must check)

### Business traveler 183-day tracker (example — Germany):

| Month | Days in Germany | Cumulative | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| January | 5 | 5 | 🟢 Safe |
| February | 8 | 13 | 🟢 Safe |
| March | 12 | 25 | 🟢 Safe |
| ... | ... | ... | ... |
| October | 15 | 120 | 🟡 Monitor |
| November | 20 | 140 | 🟡 Monitor |
| December | 45 | 185 | 🔴 183-day exceeded |

Once 183 days exceeded → Germany may tax all employment income for the year, not just days above 183.

## 5. DTA Application Process for Employers

### Step 1: Establish tax residence
Confirm where the employee is tax resident (DTA Article 4 tie-breaker if dual residence).

### Step 2: Identify income types
Classify income: employment income (Art. 15), director fees (Art. 16), equity (Art. 21 or Art. 15 depending on structure), pension (Art. 18/19).

### Step 3: Count host-country days
Track physical presence in the host country for the relevant period.

### Step 4: Apply 183-day rule
Assess whether all three conditions of Art. 15(2) are met.

### Step 5: Determine withholding obligations
Even if a DTA protects from taxation, many countries require the employer to withhold and then the employee claims a refund. Always check local withholding rules.

### Step 6: Eliminate double taxation
Apply DTA method: **exemption method** (income excluded from home-country tax base) or **credit method** (foreign tax credited against home-country tax).

Switzerland uses primarily the **exemption method with progression** (Befreiungsmethode mit Progressionsvorbehalt).

## 7. Special Topics

### Equity Compensation and DTAs

**Key issue:** When an employee receives stock options, RSUs, or shares that vest over multiple years across different countries, the income must be allocated internationally.

**OECD guidelines:** Tax is levied proportionally on the grant-to-vest working days in each country.

**Swiss position (ESTV Circular 37):** Switzerland taxes the internationally-allocated portion of equity income upon vesting (for residents) or upon exercise (for non-residents with Swiss working days).

**DTA interaction:** Most DTAs treat equity as employment income (Art. 15); the source state (country where work was performed during vest period) has primary taxing rights.

### Pension and AHV Treaty Interaction

**Swiss AHV pensions:**
- Generally exempt from Swiss withholding tax
- DTA may give Switzerland or residence state the right to tax
- Varies significantly by treaty: check DTA Art. 18/19

**Pension portability:**
- EU/EFTA: periods aggregated; pension paid from each country proportionally
- Bilateral agreements: similar aggregation rights
