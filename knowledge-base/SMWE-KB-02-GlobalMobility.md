# SMWE Knowledge Base — Module 02: Global Mobility & Assignments

**Version:** 1.0 | **Effective:** 1 August 2026 | **Next review:** 1 November 2026  
**Service codes:** GM-001 to GM-010  
**Legal sources:** EU Reg. 883/2004, ZAS bilateral agreements, AIG, VZAE, AFMP, bilateral DTAs (SIF/ESTV)

## 1. Assignment Categories

### Short-Term Assignment
- Duration: up to 12 months
- Social security: home country maintained if A1/CoC obtained
- Tax: 183-day rule frequently applies; host-country tax may not be triggered
- Payroll: typically home-country payroll; shadow payroll may be required in host country

### Long-Term Assignment
- Duration: 12 months to 5 years (typical; some up to 10 years)
- Social security: may shift to host country after agreement posting period expires
- Tax: host-country tax typically applies; full DTA analysis required
- Payroll: hybrid (home + shadow or split) or full localisation

### Permanent Transfer / Localisation
- Employee formally moved to host-country employment
- Full compliance with host-country employment law
- Home-country social security and payroll terminated (subject to portability rules)

### Local-Plus
- Employed under local terms in host country
- Selected home-country benefits preserved (e.g. pension, school fees)
- Increasingly common for assignees who do not return

## 2. A1 Certificate / Certificate of Coverage (CoC)
**GM-006**

### Purpose
The A1 certificate (within EU/EFTA under Reg. 883/2004) or Certificate of Coverage (for bilateral agreement countries) proves that the employee remains covered by the home-country social security system and is exempt from contributions in the host country.

### When Required
- Any work performed in another EU/EFTA country, regardless of duration
- Business trips of any length within EU/EFTA
- Cross-border workers performing work in their country of residence
- Assignments to bilateral agreement countries (CoC)

### Application Channels
- **Switzerland → EU/EFTA:** Apply to the competent cantonal AHV compensation office (Ausgleichskasse / caisse de compensation) or via ALPS (Automated Legal Processing System) for bulk applications
- **Form:** EE0015 (EU/EFTA A1); country-specific forms for bilateral countries
- **Timing:** Apply before the assignment or trip begins
- **Penalties:** Several countries (Germany, France, Belgium, Austria) impose on-the-spot fines for missing A1

### Duration Limits for Posted Workers
| Framework | Maximum Posting Duration |
|---|---|
| EU Reg. 883/2004 (via AFMP) | 24 months (Art. 12); extension possible by mutual agreement |
| Germany bilateral | 24 months; extension by agreement |
| US bilateral | 5 years |
| India bilateral | 60 months |
| UK (post-Brexit MOU) | 24 months |
| Countries without agreement | No posting; dual contributions may arise |

### Multi-State Workers (Art. 13 EU Reg. 883/2004)
Employees habitually working in two or more EU/EFTA states:
- Insured in country of residence if substantial activity (≥25%) performed there
- Otherwise, insured in country of registered employer
- Separate A1 required for multi-state status

## 3. Working Day Tracking
**GM-003**

### Why It Matters
Working days in a given country determine:
- Social security coverage (A1 validity, 49.9% threshold)
- DTA 183-day rule applicability
- Cross-border commuter tax status
- Posted-worker notification obligations
- Permanent establishment risk

### Day-Count Methods

**Physical presence:** Count actual calendar days with physical presence, including travel days to/from.

**Working days only:** Some DTAs specify working days (excluding travel days and rest days). Always check the specific treaty text.

**Common thresholds to monitor:**
| Threshold | Consequence |
|---|---|
| 49.9% of work time in home country | Social security shifts to home country |
| 183 days in host country (in any 12-month period) | DTA employment income article triggered |
| 90 days in Switzerland | Service notification obligation |
| 60 days in Germany for cross-border workers | Possible salary split obligation |

### Tracking Systems
- **Travel calendar / diary:** Manual log of location per day; minimum standard
- **Badge/access systems:** Objective electronic record; audit-defensible
- **Expense system integration:** Hotel/flight receipts as supporting evidence
- **Specialist tools:** Equus, BoardRoom, Topia, MoveGuides, Vialto — common in large multinationals

## 4. Shadow Payroll
**GM-004**

### Definition
A shadow payroll is a payroll run in the host country for tax purposes only. It does not result in actual payment to the employee but creates the correct tax deductions/withholdings in the host country while actual salary is paid from the home country.

### When Required
Shadow payroll is required when:
- Employee is tax-liable in the host country
- Host country mandates payroll-based withholding (e.g. tax-at-source / PAYE)
- Employer has a PE or legal presence in host country

### Switzerland-Specific
- **Inbound assignees to Switzerland:** Swiss Quellensteuer (tax-at-source) must be withheld via Swiss payroll if employee is Swiss-tax liable
- **Outbound assignees from Switzerland:** If employee becomes tax-liable abroad, foreign shadow payroll may be required in parallel with Swiss home payroll

### Shadow Payroll Components
- Gross-up calculations (if company tax equalised)
- Hypothetical tax deduction (hypo tax for tax equalisation)
- Host-country withholding tax
- Social security coordination (which system pays what)
- Year-end reconciliation and true-up

## 5. Split Payroll
**GM-005**

### Definition
Split payroll divides the employee's salary between a home-country payroll and a host-country payroll, reflecting the portion of work performed in each jurisdiction.

### When Used
- Cross-border employment with tax obligations in two countries (especially CH-DE corridor)
- Long-term assignments where employee has dual tax liability
- Local-plus arrangements

### Swiss Quellensteuer Interaction
For inbound employees to Switzerland under split payroll:
- Swiss portion of salary subject to Quellensteuer
- Foreign portion taxed in country of work
- Net income and tax equalisation calculations required
- Annual reconciliation with ESTV (ordinary reassessment if applicable under Circular 45)

## 6. International Assignment Lifecycle
**GM-009 — Expatriate Compliance**

### Pre-Departure Checklist (Switzerland outbound)
- [ ] A1/CoC application submitted and approved
- [ ] Work permit for host country secured
- [ ] Tax pre-departure review completed (residence determination)
- [ ] Swiss tax-at-source deregistration (if applicable)
- [ ] Pension fund implications reviewed (BVG continuation options)
- [ ] Hypothetical tax calculated (if tax equalisation policy)
- [ ] Cost of living allowance / COLA assessment
- [ ] Relocation support arranged
- [ ] Assignment letter / secondment agreement signed

### During Assignment
- [ ] Working day tracking ongoing
- [ ] A1 certificate on file (renewed if >24 months)
- [ ] Shadow/split payroll running correctly
- [ ] Host-country payroll compliant with local law
- [ ] Annual year-end true-up and reconciliation
- [ ] Midpoint review of visa/permit validity

### Repatriation
- [ ] Host-country tax clearance
- [ ] Swiss social security reinstatement confirmation
- [ ] Swiss payroll reactivation
- [ ] Pension fund re-affiliation
- [ ] Home-country tax return obligations in host country
- [ ] Repatriation support

## 7. Business Traveler Assessment
**GM-010**

### Risk Ladder

| Days in Host Country | Social Security | Income Tax | PE Risk | Action Required |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1–30 | A1 covers (EU/EFTA) | Treaty 183-day rule protects | Very low | A1; document travel |
| 31–60 | A1 covers | Treaty protection likely | Low | Confirm A1 validity; day log |
| 61–90 | A1 covers | Approaching 183-day zone | Low-medium | Begin formal tracking |
| 91–120 | A1 covers | 183-day risk rising | Medium | Tax analysis; possible salary split |
| 121–182 | Review if reset | Treaty protection eroding | Medium-high | DTA position paper; consider shadow |
| 183+ | Potential shift | Host-country tax triggered | High | Full compliance review; shadow/split |

### Key Countries — Business Traveler Watch Points
- **Germany:** Physical presence taxed; no formal safe harbour; strict enforcement
- **France:** Relatively pragmatic under 183-day; frontier workers separate regime
- **UK:** Statutory Residence Test applies; 183-day threshold
- **USA:** Substantial Presence Test (183-day rule variant); FICA may apply
- **India:** 182-day rule; strong enforcement for MNC employees

## 8. Home Office Abroad
**GM-002**

### General Principles
When an employee works from their home in a foreign country:
- Social security risk: shifts to home country above 49.9% working time threshold
- Income tax risk: home country may claim right to tax working days performed there
- PE risk: regular and systematic home office may create fixed place of business (OECD commentary Art. 5)
- Employment law: host country's mandatory employment law may apply

### Swiss Employer with Foreign Home-Office Employee
**Quantitative thresholds (social security — EU Reg. 883/2004):**
- Employer must monitor: if employee's home-country working time crosses 25% (soft threshold) → formal multi-state analysis required
- Hard threshold: >49.9% → social security shifts to home country → Swiss AHV/ALV stops; foreign system applies

**Impact on Swiss employer:**
- Loss of Swiss AHV/ALV contributions
- Registration obligation in employee's home country for social security
- Possible host-country employment contract obligations

### Country-Specific Home Office Rules — see KB Module 03 (Cross-Border)

## 9. Permanent Establishment Risk

### When Home Office Creates PE
Under OECD Model Art. 5, a home office may constitute a fixed place of business if:
- The employee's home is at the employer's disposal (even informally)
- The employee conducts core business functions, not merely preparatory activities
- The activity is regular and habitual, not purely incidental

**OECD 2020 Commentary:** Clarified that a home office will not typically create a PE unless the company requires the employee to work from home. However, national tax authorities interpret this differently.

### Swiss Federal Tax Administration (ESTV) Position
ESTV has not issued specific home office PE guidance post-COVID. Tax rulings are available in specific cases.

### Mitigation Strategies
- Cap foreign home-office days (employer policy: typically 20–40% maximum)
- Home-office policy document specifying business necessity
- No client meetings or commercial negotiations conducted from home
- Regular presence in Swiss office maintained
