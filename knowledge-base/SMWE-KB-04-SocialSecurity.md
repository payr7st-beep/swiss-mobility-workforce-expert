## 1. Overview: Swiss Social Security Coordination Framework

Switzerland coordinates social security through two parallel frameworks:

### Framework A — EU/EFTA Coordination (Regulation 883/2004)
Applies to all movements between Switzerland and EU/EFTA member states under the Agreement on the Free Movement of Persons (AFMP).

**Core principles:**
- **Single applicable legislation:** a person is subject to ONE state's social security only
- **Equality of treatment:** EU/EFTA nationals treated equally with Swiss nationals
- **Exportability:** pensions and benefits can be exported across borders
- **Aggregation:** contribution periods in different countries are combined

**Scope:** All branches of Swiss social security that coordinate under EU rules:
- AHV/AVS (old-age and survivors)
- IV/AI (disability)
- EO/APG (income replacement)
- ALV (unemployment)
- UVG/LAA (accidents)
- FamZ (family allowances)
- Some KVG elements for cross-border situations

**Key articles:**
- Art. 11: General rule — lex loci laboris (state of work)
- Art. 12: Posted workers — home-state exception (up to 24 months)
- Art. 13: Multi-state activity (≥25% in residence state → residence state rules)
- Art. 16: Special agreements between authorities

### Framework B — Bilateral Social Security Agreements
Applies to movements between Switzerland and non-EU/EFTA countries.

## 2. EU/EFTA Social Security Coordination — Detailed Rules

### 2.1 Lex Loci Laboris (Art. 11 Reg. 883/2004)

**Default rule:** A person who works as an employee in one EU/EFTA state is subject to that state's social security legislation.

**Application for Switzerland:**
- Employee working in Switzerland → Swiss AHV/ALV/BVG applies
- Employee working in another EU/EFTA state → that state's legislation applies
- Employer contribution follows the employee's applicable legislation

### 2.2 Posted Workers (Art. 12 Reg. 883/2004)

**Conditions for maintaining home-country coverage during posting:**
1. Employer normally carries on substantial activities in the home state (not a letter-box company)
2. Employee was subject to home-state social security immediately before posting
3. Employee is not sent to replace another posted worker
4. Posting does not exceed 24 months (Art. 12(1))

**Document:** A1 certificate issued by home-country authority

**Switzerland as posting state (outbound):**
- Swiss employer posts employee to EU/EFTA country for ≤24 months
- Employee remains in Swiss AHV/ALV system
- Swiss employer continues full AHV/ALV/BVG contributions
- A1 issued by cantonal SVA/SAK; exempts employee from host-country social security

**Switzerland as host state (inbound):**
- Foreign EU/EFTA employer posts employee to Switzerland for ≤24 months
- Employee remains in home-country system (A1 from home country)
- No Swiss AHV/ALV contributions required
- Swiss employer equivalent must accept A1 and not collect Swiss contributions

### 2.3 Multi-State Workers (Art. 13 Reg. 883/2004)

**When Art. 13 applies:** Employee habitually works in two or more EU/EFTA states.

**Determination:**

| Situation | Applicable legislation |
|---|---|
| Substantial activity (≥25%) in state of residence | State of residence |
| No substantial activity in state of residence; employer in one state | State of employer |
| No substantial activity in state of residence; employers in multiple states | State of residence |

**Substantial activity threshold:** The 25% rule is applied to working time OR remuneration. BSV typically uses working time.

**Practical impact for Swiss employers with cross-border employees doing home office:**
- If employee works ≥25% from home country → must formally determine applicable legislation
- Application to ZAS via cantonal AHV office; A1 issued confirming applicable legislation
- If Switzerland remains applicable: Swiss AHV/ALV continues
- If home country becomes applicable: Swiss contributions stop; employer registers abroad

## 4. UK-Switzerland Post-Brexit Social Security

**Framework:** UK-Switzerland Social Security Protocol (post-Brexit, AFMP no longer applies to UK)

**Scope:**
- Old-age pensions (AHV/AVS and UK State Pension)
- Disability (IV/AI and UK Personal Independence Payment)
- Survivors
- Posting of workers

**Posting:** Maximum 24 months with Certificate of Coverage (not called A1)

**Key difference from EU framework:**
- UK nationals in Switzerland no longer benefit from EU 883/2004 directly
- UK quota applies for work permits (separate from EU/EFTA)
- Frontier workers: no specific bilateral frontier worker regime (no G permit equivalent for UK-CH border)

## 5. No-Agreement Countries — Risk Assessment

When an employee moves between Switzerland and a country without a bilateral social security agreement:

**Dual contribution risk:**
- Employee may be subject to social security contributions in BOTH countries simultaneously
- Swiss AHV/ALV continues (employee works for Swiss employer)
- Host country may also mandate local social security contributions
- Combined rate can reach 30-40%+ of salary → significant cost and compliance burden

**AHV voluntary continuation:**
Swiss citizens and EU/EFTA nationals working abroad in a non-agreement country may apply for voluntary AHV continuation:
- Available within 1 year of leaving Swiss mandatory coverage
- Application to SVA Zürich (central office for voluntary insurance)
- Self-paid contributions (employee + employer share)
- Maintains Swiss pension entitlements

**Advisory steps for employers:**
1. Map posting country → check ZAS agreement database
2. Assess dual-contribution risk and cost
3. Consider AHV voluntary continuation for expat employees
4. Obtain local legal advice on host-country social security obligations
5. Include gross-up cost in assignment budget

## 6. A1 Application Flowchart

```
Is the work country in EU/EFTA?
├── YES → Apply EU Reg. 883/2004
│   ├── Single-state posting (Art. 12)? → Apply for A1 via cantonal SVA/SAK
│   ├── Multi-state activity (Art. 13)? → Determine applicable legislation; A1 reflects outcome
│   └── Cross-border worker (Art. 11)? → A1 for home-country work days if any
│
└── NO → Check bilateral agreement (ZAS database)
    ├── Agreement exists → Apply Certificate of Coverage (CoC) via cantonal SVA/SAK
    ├── No agreement → Assess dual-contribution risk; no A1/CoC available
    └── UK → Apply Certificate of Coverage under post-Brexit Protocol
```
