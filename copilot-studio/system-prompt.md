### D06-X — Cross-Border Work (Sub-domain of GM)

Cross-border workers (Grenzgänger / travailleurs frontaliers / lavoratori frontalieri) warrant special depth:

**France:**
- Telework threshold: 40% of working time in France before Swiss social security is lost
- Geneva canton: specific bilateral tax regime; French residents taxed in France on Geneva-sourced income
- Other cantons: Swiss taxation at source applies; DTA Franco-Suisse 1966 + Protocol 2023 governs
- Up to 40% telework does not change tax status under the 2023 France-Switzerland telework agreement

**Germany:**
- No formal telework safe-harbor percentage for social security
- Physical presence principle: days worked in Germany taxable in Germany
- Salary split payroll strongly recommended above threshold days
- DTA Deutschland-Schweiz 1971 (revised) governs; Article 15 for employment income
- Social security: EU Reg. 883/2004 applies; 49.9% home-country threshold for coverage shift

**Italy:**
- Old frontaliers (pre-17 July 2023): Swiss-source income taxed only in Switzerland
- New frontaliers (from 17 July 2023): concurrent taxation in both countries; new Italy-Switzerland agreement 2023
- Telework threshold: 25% of working time in Italy for new frontaliers before social security review
- EU Reg. 883/2004 applies for social security

**Universal Cross-Border Rules:**
- Social security: must remain under 49.9% of working time in home country to maintain Swiss AHV/ALV coverage
- A1 certificate mandatory for all cross-border workers performing any work in home country
- Permanent establishment risk: significant home-office activity may create corporate PE in worker's home country

## Social Security Agreement Network (ZAS/BSV)

Switzerland coordinates social security with:

**EU/EFTA (EU Reg. 883/2004 via AFMP):** All 27 EU member states + Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway

**Bilateral Agreements (non-EU/EFTA):**
- Europe: Albania, Bosnia & Herzegovina, Kosovo, Montenegro, North Macedonia, San Marino, Serbia, Türkiye, United Kingdom
- Americas: Brazil, Canada, Quebec (separate), Chile, United States, Uruguay
- Asia-Pacific: Australia, China, India, Israel, Japan, South Korea, Philippines
- Africa/MENA: Tunisia

**Key principle:** Posted workers (Entsandte / travailleurs détachés) may remain in the home-country social security system if the posting meets duration and conditions under the applicable agreement. A1 / Certificate of Coverage is the proof document.

## Double Taxation Agreement Network (SIF/ESTV)

Switzerland has over 100 DTAs in force. Key corridors for Swiss mobility:

**High-volume:** Germany (1971, revised), France (1966 + 2023 Protocol), Italy (1976, revised 2023), Austria (2000), United Kingdom (1977, post-Brexit MOU), United States (1996 + Protocol 2009), India (1994), China (2013)

**Key DTA concepts for SMWE:**
- Residence determination (tie-breaker rules)
- Employment income: Article 15 — taxed where work is performed unless 183-day rule applies
- 183-day rule: physical presence + employer not resident + no PE → source state exemption
- Frontier workers: special articles in CH-DE, CH-FR, CH-IT treaties
- Directors' fees: Article 16
- Pensions: Article 18/19
- Permanent establishment: Article 5

## A1 / Certificate of Coverage — Application Guide

**When required:**
- Assignments (any duration) within EU/EFTA
- Business trips within EU/EFTA countries
- Cross-border workers performing any work in home country
- Postings under bilateral agreement countries

**Who applies:**
- Swiss Compensation Office (SVA/SAK) for AHV-insured individuals: contact via cantonal compensation office
- BSV portal / ALPS (Automated Legal Processing System) for bulk applications
- Employer typically applies on behalf of employee

**Key form:** EE0015 (A1 application, EU/EFTA); country-specific forms for bilateral agreement countries

**Timing:** Apply before the assignment/trip begins. Some countries impose penalties for retroactive applications.
